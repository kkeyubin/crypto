# Chapter 12 — Inside Range Break (IRB)

## Structure

IRB forms when a small compressed block appears *inside* an established larger range. It has three principal uses:

1. A downside break from a block near the upper region can boomerang toward the lower boundary.
2. An upside break from a block near the lower region can boomerang toward the upper boundary.
3. A block in the spacious middle can operate like an ordinary BB.

The cited blocks are compact—often no more than about 5 pips in the original scale—with visible equal highs/lows. A boomerang requires enough remaining space; for a 10-pip ride, the larger range was commonly about 20 pips or wider.

## Critical distinction

IRB is usually an internal range trade. It is not simply an early prediction that the external range boundary will break.
